How faithfully our Sanskrit reads, akshara by akshara: our vision text, Tesseract, archive.org's text layers and the e-texts measured (FIDELITY_2026_10_22, DOCS84)

scripts/source_fidelity.py (new, read-only, free): a text's letters against an e-text (a witness in the
database or on disk, --prefix for a kanda), each reading (what we translate, the vision pages, the first
OCR, a file such as an archive.org _djvu.txt) found by anchors unique in the reference and aligned akshara
by akshara after folding what editions and typesetting change and the meaning does not; --pair A,B splits
one print's two readings into each one's own misreadings and the shared ones (another edition's reading,
the e-text's slip, a place both fail); --ledger lists every text with its source, English, judged level and
the fidelity measured. The database is opened read-only; reports go to data/source_fidelity/ (ignored).

Measured on the backup of 22 Oct: our vision text misreads or loses 3.58 aksharas in 100 of its own in the
Markandeya and 3.62 in the Satapatha's kanda 14 (Tesseract on the same pages 18.15 and 24.49); its errors
are fluent (a plausible word, a syllable written once), invisible to the judge's "cosmetic"; archive.org's
text layers of Samasrami's Satapatha agree with GRETIL in 48.94 to 55.39 of 100 aksharas; the Surya's scan
(Tesseract) in 78.79. Where an e-text exists it is the Sanskrit to translate; our scans stay as the prints.
