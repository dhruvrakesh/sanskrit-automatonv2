## 3. What it shows

- **Read side by side in the house's register, the scholarly pipeline is ahead,** by both judges, by about four units
  in five over Sonnet alone and nine in ten over flash. gemini-2.5-pro has no stake in scholar against sonnet1 (both
  Claude), so that comparison is the cleanest, and it is the clearest result here.
- **It makes the fewest major errors:** 0.24 per ten verses by both judges, against 3.6 to 3.8 for flash, the
  working English.
- **This is what BM1 could not show:** chrF put Sonnet in one pass first (nearness to 19th-century English, which the
  scholarly register leaves by design), and judge-v1 could not tell the arms apart. BM3's judges, reading the arms side
  by side against the Sanskrit, tell them apart sharply.

## 4. What it does not yet show

- **40 units is a pilot:** five a text, so no text's own picture is readable yet; the intervals above are for the 40.
- **The notes:** duel-v1 shows each translation's notes, and only the scholarly arm has notes (37 of the 40 units), so
  they name it, and a judge may rank an annotated translation higher for them. The check (BENCH_DUEL_NOTES_2026_10_16)
  reads the same units with the notes withheld: 74 readings (the three units without notes need no second reading), about $3.0 (Google $1.7). If the preference holds
  without them, it is the English's; if it shrinks, part of it was the notes.
- **Claude Opus read part of the scholarly arm before:** it audited about one sentence in ten during BM1. gemini-2.5-pro
  did not, and agrees.
- **The judges are machines:** BM2, your blind reading of the same units (the dashboard's /benchmarks/bm2), is the
  gold: it says how far each judge can be trusted.
- **The published English's low numbers are not a verdict on its translators:** the dataset pairs a line with an
  English that sometimes renders another line (the Satapatha's drift, docs/BM1_RESULTS_2026-10-16.md section 5), and
  several of them are free by intent. They stand in the table as a calibration, nothing more.
