## 3. What the numbers show

- **Nearness to the published English (chrF):** Sonnet in one pass is the closest, by a margin the intervals hold;
  the scholarly pipeline is between, ahead of flash only without the Bhagavata. Scored over each text's whole window
  (the lines joined, so that where one line's English ends and the next begins does not count), the order is the
  same overall and in six of the eight texts; in the Bhagavata the scholarly arm is last, in the Ramayana the three are
  within 0.5.
- **The arms' place in the frame:** 35.9 to 38.3, beside the Mitrasamgraha paper's 32.6 (GPT-4o), 33.4 (Claude 3.7
  Sonnet), 34.2 (Gemini 2.0 Flash), 38.4 (a fine-tuned Gemma 2 9B) and 38.8 (with retrieval). BM1 is 200 of those
  verses in our pipeline's form, with context: a frame, not a ranking.
- **judge-v1 hardly tells the arms apart:** 4.67 to 4.87 of 5, 92 to 95% passed. Its differences are small (0.2) and
  it is not neutral: it is gemini-2.5-flash judging, among others, gemini-2.5-flash's English, and the scholarly arm was
  revised against it until it passed. Its order (scholar and flash ahead of sonnet1 on fidelity) runs against chrF's.
- **judge-v1 is hard on the translators of record:** it passes fewer than half of the published English. Part of that
  is theirs (the Bhagavata's and the Yogavasistha's English are free and expansive); part may be the judge's (it
  passes Buhler's close Manu at 88%, so it rewards a literal, complete rendering, which is the house's aim, but a
  judge cannot certify itself).

## 4. What they do not show

- That the scholarly pipeline is worth eight times flash's price, or Sonnet's: on these 200 verses neither measure
  shows it ahead of both. Nor that it is not: chrF measures nearness to 19th-century English, which the scholarly
  register (modern prose, names in IAST, notes apart) leaves by design, and judge-v1 is saturated and partial.
- Which arm is the most faithful to the Sanskrit: that is what BM3 asks (the arms side by side, blind, by judges of
  both families, in the Debroy register of scholar_duel.py's duel-v1), and BM2 decides (a person's reading).
- No change to the pipeline follows from BM1 alone, as agreed ("establish a worthy baseline"). The questions it puts:
  whether the working English should be Sonnet's rather than flash's (chrF for it, judge-v1 against it), and where the
  scholarly pipeline earns its price.

## 5. A correction to the method page

docs/BENCH_MITRA_2026-10-16.md (section 4) said the Satapatha's pairs are shifted and that the report marks such
units. On the full run the test marks none of the 200. The pairs drift rather than shift one for one: line 156's
published English renders lines 155 and 156 together, and line 155's seems to close the sentence before; a
neighbour's English, longer, does not fit the arms' English better by 10 points (chrF weighs recall). That text's
chrF is therefore lower for every arm alike, and the paired differences stand; the whole-window chrF above, which a
boundary does not touch, orders the arms as before. BENCH_DUEL_2026_10_16 adds this to the method page.
