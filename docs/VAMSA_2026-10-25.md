## 2. The check (scripts/vamsa_check.py, free)

- **The citation, found.** Each Purana Index citation is parsed (the text, the book, the chapter, the verses: "63-5"
  is 63 to 65; a bare "50. 78" takes the text of the citation before it, as the index prints them) and the verse is
  found in GRETIL's text. The Brahmanda: the index's pada II is GRETIL's part 1 (its colophon at 1,14.75 reads
  "purvabhage dvitiye 'nusangapade ... caturdaso 'dhyayah"), III is part 2 (2,2.32 "madhyabhage trtiya upoddhatapade"),
  IV part 3; measured, this mapping finds more names than the other. The Visnu is read in both editions.
- **Read.** The cited verse and the two either side of it (the index's edition and GRETIL's may number a verse
  apart): both names stand there, one of them, or neither.
- **How a name is found.** By its letters as compared: the vowels by quality (a and long a are one; e, o and r are
  not), the anusvara and a nasal before a stop as one sign, the visarga left out, an avagraha read as its a; the name
  less its last vowel at the start of a word; in the cited window also inside a word of four letters or more (the
  Venkatesvara prints join the sandhi: tasmaccandrah). A name of two consonants or fewer (Aja, Anu, Kusa) by its case
  forms (ajah, ajad, ajasya; the neuter plural left out, so Bhuta is not found in bhutani). A bracketed part is tried
  with and without it (Vidyu(t)jjihva); a second form the index gives in brackets too (Ghrta (Dhrta)).
- **Tuned on the real data, for precision.** A first version compared consonants only; it counted Daksa as found in
  "narisyantad rksah" and Kusadhvaja in "kesidhvajah". The vowels now count: those are "one name", and the verse is
  shown, so the reader sees the edition's reading.
- **The epics.** For every relation of the three indexes: the verses of the Mahabharata, the Harivamsa and the Ramayana
  in which both names stand at the start of a word (how many, and the first).
- **What it writes.** data\shelf\vamsa.db (vamsa_rel: one row a relation; vamsa_verses: the TEI verses kept to show;
  vamsa_texts: the editions and terms; vamsa_meta: the stamps), exports\vamsa_checks.csv, exports\vamsa_summary.json.
  The maintenance task runs it with --scheduled (step d): it writes only when kin.db or the zip changed.

## 4. The joins (scripts/vamsa_view.py)

A line is joined only through the index's own entries, as KIN_LINES_2026_10_24 joins it (the homonym's number, the one
entry of the name, the one homonym that names the other). Of the 14,088 parent relations kept, 7,468 had a person the
rule could not tell apart. Two more joins, each shown on the step it makes:

- **The same rule, the honorific aside** (586 sides): "Sri Rama" is Rama; a homonym that names the other once the
  honorifics are set aside.
- **By a shared verse** (891 sides): of the entries of the name, the one whose own entry cites a verse this relation
  cites. For Soerensen the printed reference itself is compared (I, 66, 2616).

The rest (7,607 sides) stay "a name only": a leaf of their own relation, never joined to another.

## 5. The lines

The longest lines, from a person without a parent in the index down the child whose own line runs longest: the Purana
Index's thirty longest run 38, 34, 30, 30, 26 ... generations; Soerensen has 20 of six or more (24, 19, 17 ...); the
Vedic Index none.

The line through **Dasaratha (II)** in the Purana Index runs 38 generations, from Asmaka and Mulaka through Raghu, Aja,
Dasaratha, Rama, Kusa, Atithi and the later Iksvakus to Brhadbala (VI): 29 of its 37 steps have both names in the
cited verse, 6 one name, 2 no citation; 30 steps are joined by the index's rule and 7 by a shared verse; 28 are stated
in both entries. The line through **Janamejaya (I)** runs down through Satanika (II), Sahasranika, Asvamedhaja and
Asimakrsna to Sunaya (I): the Paurava kings after the war, each step with its verse.

Where the index states two parents of whom both could be the father (a ksetraja son, begotten by another on a king's
wife, is called the king's and the begetter's), the page draws the line up through the parent the index marks male,
stated in both entries, whose own line is longest; it names the other parents beside the person and draws the line
through any of them on request ("line through"). The line up from Asmaka goes to Vasistha (IV), as the index states
it.

## 6. The page (/vamsa on the dashboard)

- Search the index's own head words (most of its persons are not among our names); the indexes number the persons who
  share a name, and each is shown apart. The longest lines are listed by index.
- A ladder of generations: each person with spouses, siblings and other parents; between two generations the step's
  marks: verse (both names), one name, not held, the epics' counts (MBh 4), our own passages, Raychaudhuri, and how the
  step was joined (a shared verse, a name only).
- The step's evidence: the index's words and printed reference, the Sanskrit verse with the two names marked and its
  edition, the epics' verses that name both (the Harivamsa's and the Ramayana's verse shown; the Mahabharata's by
  reference only), our passages, Raychaudhuri's view.
- "Ask the Professor about this step" puts a question on the Ask page with the Professor chosen; it is not asked until
  the reader asks it (each answer is metered under the cap).
- Links: the dashboard's top bar, the Library's header, the Relations page (beside each line of descent), the Ask page.
- Read-only: context.db, kin.db and vamsa.db opened mode=ro; no model is called.

## 7. What it cannot yet say

- A word match is not a reading: "both names in the verse" says where to read. A different reading in the edition is
  "one name"; sandhi that changes a name's first letter (tasmac chucih for Suci) is not seen.
- The Vayu is not held; Soerensen's numbers are the Calcutta edition's, so his relations are checked only by verses of
  the critical edition that name both.
- The joins are conservative: 7,607 sides are names only. Many of those persons are in the genealogical chapters of the
  Puranas, which we hold in Sanskrit but have not translated (section 8).
- No inference is drawn by a model on this page yet (section 8, G2).
