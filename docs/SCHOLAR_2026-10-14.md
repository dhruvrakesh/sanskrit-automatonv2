## 1. The question, and the answer

Asked on 14 Oct: *"we need accurate authoritative high fidelity Debroy-esque translations with all the reiteration
and expense and context. Is this done in full?"*

**No.** What exists is a working English:
- one pass, by `gemini-2.5-flash`;
- the prompt names the Debroy tradition (`v3-2026-09-27`), and gives the five verses before as context;
- no glossary is kept from verse to verse, no notes are written, nothing reads a verse again;
- a judge reads a sample of 2 to 5% afterwards.

It is a good draft. It is not what a Debroy-grade translation is:
- every word of the critical text rendered, with nothing added;
- names and terms kept in Sanskrit and explained in notes, one spelling throughout;
- the reading of a verse checked against its neighbours;
- revised until it is right.

**Two examples from MBh01, chapter 1, in the working English now:**
- **1.1.8** reads "near Janamejaya ... and Pārikṣita (son of Parīkṣit)", as if two men. *pārikṣitasya* is
  Janamejaya's own patronymic: he is the son of Parikṣit.
- **1.1.5** reads "Lomaharṣaṇa took the seat". *lomaharṣaṇiḥ* is Lomaharṣaṇa's son (Ugraśravas), the narrator.

A critic reading the verse word by word catches both. The working English has no critic.

## 2. What this release does

### The scholarly English (`scripts/scholar_translate.py`)

A second English beside the working one. The working English (`passages.translation`) is never touched.

**Each verse is sent with:**
- the verse and its IAST;
- the 6 verses before it, Sanskrit and English: the scholarly English where it passed, the working English
  otherwise (marked as a draft that may be wrong);
- the 2 verses after it (Sanskrit), for a sentence that runs on;
- the text, the source (for MBh01: the BORI Critical Edition e-text), the chapter and the verse;
- the glossary of the names and terms in these verses (up to 40). It comes from three places:
  - the entities found in them (`entity_mentions`; a spelling only: the prompt says it does not tell who is who);
  - the entries earlier verses added (only verses that passed add);
  - a person's entries, which win.

**The passes (gemini-2.5-pro, JSON):**
1. **The draft** (`scholar-v1`), temperature 0.1. The rules:
   - every word, nothing added; agents of absolutives resolved; a patronymic is the same person;
   - plain modern prose, no archaism; names in IAST as the glossary spells them;
   - terms in IAST with a note on first use; brackets only for implied words;
   - notes for epithets, allusions, terms, readings, textual problems; doubts said;
   - one verse in, one verse out;
   - "translate afresh; never quote or reconstruct any published translation".
   Out: the translation, the notes, new glossary entries, doubts.
2. **The critic** (`scholar-critic-v1`), temperature 0. It gives:
   - fidelity and completeness, 1 to 5;
   - each issue: omission, addition, misreading, grammar, name, term, register, note or source, with where, the
     fix, major or minor;
   - accept or revise.
3. **A revision** when the critic asks, and the critic again: up to `--rounds 2` revisions.
4. **The judge** (`judge_sample.py`'s judge-v1, gemini-2.5-flash) scores the scholarly English **and the working
   English of the same verse**, so the two can be compared verse by verse.

**The verdict:**
- **passed**: the critic accepts the last version, and the judge gives 4 or more for fidelity and fluency;
- **flagged**: a person reads it;
- **source doubt**: the critic says the Sanskrit itself looks corrupt or misread; another edition or a better scan
  is wanted for that verse.

**Not sent, counted and listed:**
- noise and front matter;
- fewer than 8 Devanagari signs;
- OCR debris (Latin letters inside the Devanagari: mend the reading first; `redeem.py`'s rule);
- a reading below quality 0.35;
- numbers among the words: an index, errata, an apparatus or a notation, not verse. Verse numbers between dandas
  and a sutra's own number do not count;
- Hindi prose (three or more Hindi function words): a bilingual edition's own Hindi.

A text withdrawn, set aside or in repair is refused whole (exit 3).

**Stored (append-only, a new version each time):**
- `scholar_translations`: one row a verse a version, with:
  - the translation, notes, doubts, the first draft, every critique, the passes;
  - the judge's scores for both Englishes, the status, the engines, the prompt versions, the cost;
  - `src_hash`, the Sanskrit it was made from. A later change of the reading makes the row **stale**, not wrong;
  - `reviewed_by`, `reviewed_at`, `review_verdict`, `review_note`: a person's word (`--review`).
- `scholar_glossary`: the entries passed verses added, and a person's (`--term`).
- `data/scholar/<doc>_<stamp>.jsonl`: each run's log.

**Spend:** `usage_log`, kind `scholar` (the translator and the critic) and `judge`, from the provider's token counts.
So the spend cap counts it. `redeem.py`'s ledger counts kind `translation` only.

**Safe by default:**
- a dry run unless `--yes`: what it sends, what it leaves out, what it costs (free, read-only);
- `--show-prompt` prints the first verse's whole prompt;
- `--yes` is capped by `--max-usd` (it can pass it by one call at most) and by the spend cap;
- it stops while the dashboard runs a job that changes this text's Sanskrit or English. A Hindi job does not stop it;
- without a key, nothing is called and the database is not opened for writing.

**The cost:**
- about **$0.05 a verse**, up to $0.10 with two revisions. That is a prior until 10 verses are measured; then the
  table's own mean is used;
- the pilot below: about $1.23 for 24 verses;
- for scale: MBh01's 6,957 verses would be about $355; all 23,265 English verses of the corpus about $1,190.

**What a person does:**
- `--report` writes `exports\scholar_<doc>.html`;
- the dashboard shows `/scholar` and `/scholar/<doc>` (read-only). Each verse shows:
  - the Sanskrit and IAST beside the scholarly English, with numbered notes;
  - the doubts;
  - under a fold: the working English, the first draft, every critique, the judge's reason;
- `--review 1.1.8,1.1.9 --verdict accept --by NAME` records a person's word on the current version;
- `--term T --rendering R --kind K --note N` adds a glossary entry that wins over the others.

- else, for a text the path has not charted, the count: to translate again, a source first.

The waiting cards (no English yet) hold the same way. Chart again on The path after a repair, and the buttons
come back.

**translate_passages.py:**
- a whole-text run on a text the register has withdrawn or set aside is refused before anything is sent:
  "REFUSING (HOLD_2026_10_14)";
- exit **4**, so that the Library does not take it for the debris guard's hold (exit 3);
- `--allow-held` or `SA_ALLOW_HELD=1` lets it through; one page (`--only-page`, as T2 uses) is never held;
- the dashboard's job verdict says "held", not "failed".

### MBh01's prose (MBH_PROSE_2026_10_14)

**What is missing.** MBh01 lacks every prose passage of the critical edition:
- 1.1.1 (*lomaharṣaṇaputra ugraśravāḥ sautiḥ ...*);
- 1.3.2 to 1.3.59 (the Pauṣya story), 1.4.1-3, most of chapter 90, and the rest.

**Why.** The e-text's own readme (John Smith, MBh00.txt) says a prose line's last letter is a capital
(`01003001A`). `import_mbh_gretil.py` read `[a-z]` only, so it passed over every prose line and kept the
speaker's line above it: 1.3.1 is "sūta uvāca" alone.

**The fix:**
- the importer reads the capital, and the BORI form of the reference as well as GRETIL's;
- it marks those passages prose, and says how many prose lines it read and how many reference-like lines it did not;
- `reingest_pages.py --no-segment` compares an e-text one record a passage, as MBh01 went in. The verses keep their
  English, and the prose comes in untranslated. The plan shows it before anything is written;
- the NEXT lines of `reingest_pages.py` now carry `--merged-dir` (and `--no-segment`) when they were given, so a
  pasted `--apply` reads the same pages the plan read.

**The terms.** BORI's notice on the e-text: "Please do not provide copies of the text to others". The site shows
MBh01's Sanskrit. Whether that is within BORI's terms is a question for you, or for BORI; this release does not
decide it.
