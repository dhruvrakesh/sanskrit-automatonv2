# How faithfully our Sanskrit reads: our scans, archive.org's text layers and the e-texts (22 Oct 2026)

Marker: `FIDELITY_2026_10_22`. Tool: `scripts/source_fidelity.py` (new; read-only, free). Tests:
`tests/test_source_fidelity_2026_10_22.py` (20). The numbers below are from the backup of 22 Oct, 09:18 IST
(`context_pre_witness_satapatha_madhyandina__w_gretil_20261022_091848.db`), opened immutable, and the page files on
the PC. Nothing was written to the database.

## 1. The question, and the answer in short

*Are our own OCR and vision readings better than archive.org's, and is the Sanskrit we translate faithful enough, or
should GRETIL, the djvu texts and the other e-texts replace it?*

- **Where it could be measured, our vision text is far better than archive.org's text layer.** archive.org's readings
  of Samasrami's Satapatha (Bibliotheca Indica, Calcutta, 1903 to 1911) agree with GRETIL in 49 to 55 of every 100
  aksharas of the kandikas they can be found in at all, and only about a third of the kandikas in their volumes can
  be found. Tesseract on our own kanda-14 scan agrees in 58; our vision text of the same pages in 82.
- **Our vision text is close, not faithful.** Measured against a second reading of the same page (Tesseract's), so that
  another edition's readings do not count against it, the vision text misreads or loses **3.6 aksharas in 100** of its
  own: in the Markandeya (3.58) and in the Satapatha's kanda 14 (3.62). That is about one akshara in each sloka.
  Tesseract, on the same pages, misreads 18 (the Markandeya) and 24 (the Satapatha).
- **The e-texts are better than our vision text where they exist.** They were typed and checked by people against a
  named or a known print; their letters are their edition's. The exceptions are known: GRETIL's Satapatha lost every
  "ch" and has no kanda 12; the Surya's e-text writes a final consonant apart from the vowel after it.
- **The judge's "cosmetic" does not mean faithful.** The judge (`repair_text.py --judge`) and the noise measure
  (`etext_witness.quality`) see Latin debris and malformed words. Vision's errors are well-formed Devanagari: a
  plausible word for the printed one, a syllable written once where the print has it twice. Both texts judged
  "cosmetic" (the Markandeya at 0.2% misread, the Satapatha at 1.8%) carry the 3.6 in 100. QUALITY_METHODOLOGY's
  "known blind spot" holds for vision OCR too.

## 2. What is measured, and how

`source_fidelity.py --ref REFERENCE --cand READING [--cand ...] [--pair A,B]`

- **The reference** is an e-text: a witness in the database (`db:markandeya_purana__w_gretil`) or on disk
  (`witness:satapatha_madhyandina__w_gretil`, its appendix left out), cut into its own units (verses, kandikas);
  `--prefix 14.` keeps one kanda.
- **A reading** is what we translate (`db:<doc>`), the vision pages (`vision:<doc>`, `data/raw_vision`), the first OCR
  (`raw:<doc>`, `data/raw`, Tesseract), or a file (`file:<path>`, an archive.org `_djvu.txt`).
- **Folding, on both sides alike** (what the editions and the typesetting change and the meaning does not): spaces,
  punctuation, dandas, numbers, Latin letters, accents, avagraha and nukta dropped; a nasal before a consonant, a final
  m and the candrabindu read as the anusvara; a final consonant and the vowel after it written as one (prag astam as
  pragastam), never across a danda; a colon after a letter read as the visarga (Tesseract writes one); with
  `--fold-ch`, ccha and cha read as ca (for GRETIL's Satapatha).
- **Counted in aksharas** (a consonant cluster with its vowel sign, or a vowel, with its anusvara or visarga), so that
  a dropped "sta" is one error, not three letters.
- **Finding a unit:** 7-letter anchors unique in the reference vote for one place in the reading; the first and the
  last anchor bound it. Where the reading gives the unit twice (the mula, then its words with the sandhi undone; a
  commentary quoting it whole), it is read at both places and the better kept.
- **Aligning:** difflib's matcher, akshara by akshara. Its edit script is never shorter than the true edit distance,
  so every error rate here is an upper bound. What the reading has before a unit's first akshara and after its last is
  not counted. A unit is "found" when at least half its aksharas are matched.
- **One print, two readings (`--pair`):** over the units both read, an akshara of the e-text that one reading lacks
  (misread or missing) and the other has is the first's **own** error; one both lack is **shared**: the print's own
  reading (another edition), a slip of the e-text, or a place both OCRs fail (a broken letter, a ligature both
  misread). Aksharas a reading adds are counted apart, because where they go is less sure.
- **The report** of each run is `data/source_fidelity/<reference>[__<prefix>]__<first reading>.json` (every unit's
  agreement per reading, the commonest differences, the worst units, the pairs); git ignores the folder, as it does
  `data/source_judged/`: the reports are made again. `--ledger` reads them.
- **The steps of the verdict.** With a pair: own misreadings at most 1 in 100 *faithful*, 5 *close*, 12 *usable*, more
  *damaged*. Without one, by agreement with the e-text: 97% *faithful*, 90% *close*, 80% *differs* (misreadings and
  another edition cannot be told apart: measure a second reading), less *damaged*. Set on the three texts below.

## 4. What the errors are

- **Vision (Gemini 2.5 Flash):** fluent errors. A syllable written once where the print has it twice (`jantunam
  mamata` read `jantunam mata`), a plausible word for the printed one (`gatvayodhyajanam` read `gatva yojanam`, "the
  people of Ayodhya" become "a yojana"; `paurtam` read `paushtam`; `sapakshabhyam` read `sapadbhyam`), a cluster dropped
  (`prishtastabhih` read `prishtabhih`). Tesseract, noisy as it is, has the printed reading at most of these places.
  A translator reading the vision text translates the wrong word, fluently.
- **Both OCRs at one place** (the shared column): the old founts' ligatures, `tra` read as `ca` (the Markandeya: 27
  times in vision, 72 in Tesseract), the repha, a final virama.
- **Tesseract:** shape confusions (`ai` read `e`, `dha` read `gha`, `ha` read `da`), the repha lost.
- **archive.org's text layer of the 1903 to 1911 Calcutta print:** the same shape confusions, more of them (`vai` read
  `ve` most of all), and two of every three kandikas too garbled to be found.
- **The e-texts:** GRETIL's Satapatha lost every "ch" (`gacati` for `gacchati`; folded here), has no kanda 12, and
  writes the Vajasaneyi's sandhi as Weber printed it; the Surya's e-text writes a final consonant apart from its vowel
  (folded here).

## 5. What it means, text by text (the decisions are yours)

1. **The Surya Siddhanta.** Our scan is Tesseract's (78.79%, no vision pages); the e-text is already the text of record
   (released 21 Oct, mended in place). Keep the scan as the print, off the translation path. *Regenerate fully:* the
   e-text's working English (515 verses, about $0.30 at October's $0.00058 a passage), then its names and vectors.
2. **The Markandeya.** Our vision text (adhyayas 1 to 15) is close (3.58 own in 100). GRETIL's (adhyayas 1 to 93,
   its header credits www.sansknet.org) is the better text to translate: typed, longer, and released. *Regenerate fully:*
   GRETIL's working English for its 5,240 passages (about $3.04), names (about $1.00) and vectors (about $0.08), about
   $4.12 in all against the $4.42 left under the $85 Google cap on 22 Oct (`set_budget.py` shows it; raising it is
   yours). Its 1,408 scholarly English passages stay. Our scan stays as the print witness; its English stays, marked as
   the English of a reading with about one misread akshara a sloka.
3. **The Satapatha, kanda 14.** Our text (the Brihadaranyaka's adhyayas, 14.4 to 14.9, with a commentary) is close
   (3.62 own in 100) and has what GRETIL lacks: the ch, the commentary. Keep it. GRETIL's kanda 14 has 14.1 to 14.3,
   which our scan lacks, and the cleaner mula elsewhere. The best reading per kandika is R4 (the distillate), not a
   replacement. GRETIL's Satapatha stays a local witness until its ch is restored.
4. **Samasrami's text layers from archive.org.** Not a text to translate and not a reliable source for the ch: a word's
   ch can be taken from them only where the word is found and read whole. D1b therefore changes: the ch from our own
   vision reading of Samasrami's page images (if you download the volumes' PDFs; vision at October's $0.0032 a page),
   or from a list of the ch-words in our other texts, each checked.
5. **The other 61 texts** have no letter-level measure yet. GRETIL's index (fetched 22 Oct) lists e-texts for three of
   them: the Nirukta (`sa_yAska-nirukta`), the Rgveda (Aufrecht's, `sa_Rgveda-edAufrecht`) and the Samaveda
   (`sa_sAmavedasaMhitA`); the Taittiriya and Vajasaneyi Samhitas are TITUS's, restricted. Each becomes a witness the
   usual way (etext_witness.py: preview, write, ingest), then is measured here.
6. **The fairest test is still to come:** archive.org's own text layer of the very scan we read, the Digital Library of
   India's 2015.405693 (our Satapatha) and 2015.368408 (our Natyasastra), measured with `--pair` against our text. The
   RUNBOOK gives the listing; downloading is yours.

## 6. Limits

- The agreement mixes misreadings with another edition's readings; only the pair separates them, and the shared column
  still holds places where both OCRs fail together.
- difflib's alignment gives upper bounds; the true distance can be a little lower.
- A unit is found by anchors unique in the reference; a reading too garbled to share them (Tesseract on a damaged page,
  archive.org's Samasrami) is "not found", and its agreement is over what was found.
- Three texts are measured; the steps (1, 5 and 12 own in 100; 97, 90 and 80% agreement) are set on them.
- The meter's vision price in October is $0.0032 a page (2,987 calls, $9.57). `repair_text.py` prices a repair at the
  text's own measured rate (20 delivered pages or more), else at the last 300 vision calls corpus-wide, and at its
  fallback of $0.0004 a page only where nothing is metered. The Surya's "about $0.02" for 55 pages was written on 12
  Oct, before REINGEST_2026_10_12 priced repairs from the meter (corrected by DOCS85_2026_10_22; this page said the
  fallback wrote it): a judged file carries the price of its day; judge it again for today's (about $0.18 at
  October's rate).
