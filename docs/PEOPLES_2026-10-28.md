# The peoples of the Puranas' lists, read against Raychaudhuri (28 Oct 2026)

PEOPLES_2026_10_28 (`scripts\peoples_index.py`). The ask, as recalled on 28 Oct: original ideas and correlations from
our additional translations, carrying Raychaudhuri's work forward, and correlating it with the tribes of ancient
India, as B. C. Law wrote of them. The asks register had not recorded it in those words (docs\ASKS_2026-10-22.md,
section 14). This page says what is built, what it found, and what is still missing.

## 1. What is built, and what it rests on

`peoples_index.py` is free and read-only. It calls no model, opens every database read-only, and writes only with
`--yes`: `data\shelf\peoples_index.json` and `.csv`. It stays on this PC, as the Cologne shelf does.

## 2. What it found

Run on 28 Oct against the 17:48 backup (read-only, immutable), with `data\shelf\claims.db` and GRETIL's zip.

- **The lists:** 289 peoples, from 310 of Dikshitar's entries.
  - By list: the Matsya names 115, the Brahmanda 144, the Visnu 20 and the Vayu 137.
  - 91 stand in two lists or more; 4 stand in all four.
  - 76 stand in the Vayu's list alone, so they wait for a Vayu text.
- **Found in the verse Dikshitar cites, or within two verses of it:**
  - Matsya: 109 of 115.
  - Brahmanda: 131 of 144.
  - Visnu: 19 of 20.
  - Of the 26 not found, most are a different reading in GRETIL's text:
    - *kosalah* with a dental s (M 53);
    - *karingah* for Kalinga (Br 42);
    - *kamvojah* for Kambhoja (Br 49);
    - *caitkalaih*, probably a misprint of *cotkalaih* (my reading), at Br 63;
    - Kasmira, cited at Br 47, stands at Br 51.
- **The two Puranas compared:** the Matsya and the Brahmanda both place 50 peoples. They agree on the region of 47 and differ on 3:
  - **Kulya.** The Matsya puts it in the madhyadesa list (35). The Brahmanda puts it in the southern list (56). In the Brahmanda's madhyadesa list the matching place reads *kusalyah sausalyah* (41). The Matsya also has a southern Kulya (46), but Dikshitar does not cite it.
  - **Magadha (Magadhas).** The Matsya puts it in the eastern list (45, *salvamagadhagonardah*). The Brahmanda puts it at the close of the madhyadesa list (42, *magadhas cotkalaih saha*), where the Matsya reads *mukas caivandhakaih saha* (36). The Brahmanda also has Magadha in the east (55).
  - **Utkala.** In the Matsya it is in the Vindhya list (52). In the Brahmanda it is at the close of the madhyadesa list (42); its Vindhya list (63) reads *caitkalaih*.
- **Ours:** 113 peoples are tied to our names, and our texts name 111 of them.
- **The historian:** Raychaudhuri's Part I speaks of 39 of the peoples, in 371 claims. The relations most often given:
  - "other": 110;
  - king of: 56;
  - located in: 45;
  - capital of: 23;
  - identified with: 22.

## 3. Raychaudhuri and the lists, side by side (a reading of twelve; the tool infers nothing)

His modern geography cannot be matched to the Puranas' regions by machine, so this section is my reading of the rows,
for twelve peoples:

| Videha | M 45, Br 54: pracya | p. 18: "roughly the modern Tirhut in Bihar"; p. 19: capital Mithila |
| Malla | Br 55: pracya | p. 64: capital Kusavati or Kusinara |

| Surasena | M 35, Br 41: madhyadesa | p. 71: capital Mathura on the Yamuna |
| Avanti | M 54, Br 65: vindhyaprstha | p. 75: Mahismati its capital (the Mahagovinda Suttanta) |
| Andhra | Br 59: daksinapatha | p. 44: the deltas of the Godavari and the Krishna |
| Sabara, Pulinda, Vaidarbha | M 47-48: the southern and western lists; Br 58: daksinapatha | p. 44: "described as Daksinapathavasinah in the Matsya and Vayu Puranas" |

The last row is a check the tool makes possible. Raychaudhuri cites the Matsya's word for these three peoples, and
GRETIL's Matsya prints them under that word (114.46-48). The others above are consistent too, as I read them: in each,
his location falls inside the list's region.

**One lead:** the Brahmanda names *pancalas* in its northern list (Br 46: *aparantas ca muhmas ca pancalas
carmamandalah*). Everywhere else the Pancalas are of the middle region, as Raychaudhuri (p. 31) and the lists' own
Kuru-Pancala have them. At the matching place the Matsya (40) reads *puramdhras caiva sudras ca pallavas
cattakhandikah*. Whether Br 46 is a variant or a corruption is a question for other editions, and for the Vayu
(45.11x), which we do not hold.

## 4. How a name is read in the lists (and why not as vamsa_check reads it)

The lists join peoples in compounds: *kurupancalah*, *kasikosalah*, *salvamagadhagonardah*. A name is therefore
looked for in these ways, the best first, across the whole verse:

1. a case form of the name;
2. the last member of a compound (*sajangalah*, *sahakacchah*);
3. the first member (*kasikosalah*, *barbarascaiva*);
4. a member inside a compound, after a member's vowel or a visarga's sandhi (*kupathastanganah*);
5. the name after a vowel's sandhi (*cotkalaih* for Utkala; *caivandhakaih* for Andhaka);
6. last, `vamsa_check.py`'s strict reading.

Two further rules:

- b and v are one, because the e-texts confuse them (*vahlikah* for Bahlika).
- The words that open or close a region are never taken for a people. Otherwise the Matsya's *ity ete aparantas tu*
  would make "Aparanta" a people of the western list; Br 46 does name a people Aparanta, in the northern list.

`vamsa_check.py`'s loose reading is not used. It accepts a short name's form after any consonant, so in these lists it
read the *anga* of *tanganah* as the Angas. Its skeleton also drops an m before a stop even across a vowel, which
leaves Magadha only two consonants, so Magadha is never found inside *magadhagonardah*. That is recorded for the Vamsa
page (vamsa-skeleton, section 5), not changed here.
