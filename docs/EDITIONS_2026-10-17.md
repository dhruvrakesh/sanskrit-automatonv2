# Scholarly books from the corpus: what is ready, what is not, and the edition's format (17 Oct 2026)

Asked on 17 Oct: "can we have some authoritative and scholarly books from our corpus in our good html format like
ganita yuktibhasa and a few others, which have little to no pending work, debris". The format is `export_html.py`'s
edition (the Export button, Trilingual: the Sanskrit, its IAST, the English side by side, the notes at the end of each
chapter). SCHOLAR_EDITION_2026_10_17 gives it the scholarly English. The counts below are from the daily backup
(`D:\backups\context_daily.db`, 16 Oct 22:31 UTC), read with `mode=ro&immutable=1`.

backup (the desk's "Make the edition" makes it again from the database itself, free):
- 1,431 passages in scholarly English (its 1,430 verse passages and one more): 1,195 passed, 214 flagged by the judge, 22
  doubted for the reading; 1,409 with notes (3,876 notes and doubts in all); none yet read by a person;
- made by Claude Sonnet 5.5 (prompt scholar-v2.1 with the critic and the Agama rules), judged by gemini-2.5-flash;
- its reading is 98.5% vision OCR (the repair of 15 Oct); the register holds it in repair (off the site);

  before them; one verse with the working English, marked.
What it needs to be a book a person can stand behind: the 236 flagged and doubted verses read (Triage, free; then
`scholar_translate.py --review`), and a person's sample of the passed. Its sections are pages, not patalas: the
exporter groups by a numeric chapter, which this text's passages do not carry (a later step, free).

**Two findings:**
- **The Ganita Yukti Bhasa is not yet a sound book.** Its export is the one praised, but its reading is Tesseract's
  alone: 474 of its 2,372 English passages carry [ILLEGIBLE], and 494 of its "Sanskrit" passages are English or
  numbers read as Devanagari. About 1,880 are clean Sanskrit prose (the Sanskrit rendering of Jyesthadeva's work,
  well translated where the reading is clean). The register's "complete" (11 Oct: "what is left is not Sanskrit")
  was about the untranslated rest, not about the translated part. Its scan is here (339 page PDFs in the inbox, 339 read by Tesseract, none by vision): a vision repair
  (about $1.4 at the corpus price, up to $3.0 at the Patala Khanda's measured $0.0087 a page), the re-ingest of the
  changed pages, then its English again.
- **The working English of a "done" text can be badly wrong where the checks pass it.** The Markandeya Purana,
  page 4, passage 8 (`तं दृष्ट्वा बलवद्वज्रमुत्थितं ...`: as I read it, the celestial women, seeing the wielder of
  the mighty vajra risen from his seat, bowed to the divine sage; a person should confirm) is rendered by flash with an invented and obscene gloss of "vajra"; its QA score is 1.0, the path
  says done, and it is on the site. The scholarly English or a person's reading is what catches such a verse.

## 3. The edition (SCHOLAR_EDITION_2026_10_17)

- `python scripts\export_html.py --db data\context.db --doc <doc> --scholar --sanskrit --side-by-side`, or Export As:
  "The scholarly edition", or the desk's "Make the edition (free)" on a text with scholarly English;
  `/desk/<doc>/edition` opens the newest.
- Each verse's current scholarly row (as the prose page reads it), kept only while the Sanskrit it was made from is
  the Sanskrit here; a sentence made over several verses once, after its verse range; its notes and its doubts about
  the reading as the chapter's numbered notes; a dagger on what is flagged or doubted and not yet accepted by a
  person; the working English, marked as a draft, where a person rejected the scholarly English or there is none.
- The file gains `_scholar`: the working edition is never overwritten. Read only; free.
