Thus, the text on archery, as spoken by Ish, is a composition derived from the sayings of the Dhanurveda and the Rhyasa. It has been created by me, without the Shazghar, for no one else is knowledgeable about the essence of the Dhanurveda. Therefore, it is to be planted in the doubt-laden mind. In this text, there are also reflections on the valorous Chintamani. By the grace of agricultural practice, they are produced. The Jeta should be designated for the practice of the Parasenya. If there is even one renowned archer in a city, then the animals will flee far from the lion's den. The method of archery is well established in the Brahmana. It is not given to the cunning or the dull-witted. The sword should be given to the Brahmana, and the club to the courtesan, while the club should be given to the Shudra. Thus, the sword, the club, and the sharp club are the seventh external combat. There are seven types of combat, and the Acharya should be the one to teach them. The four types of combat are Bharghava, and from the Drabhya, there should be one warrior. The hand should be the measure. Pushya, Rohini, Uttarashadha, and Revati are the ten nakshatras. In the third, sixth, seventh, and tenth, as well as in the eleventh, all lunar activities should be performed. The third, fifth, seventh, and tenth should be noted.
